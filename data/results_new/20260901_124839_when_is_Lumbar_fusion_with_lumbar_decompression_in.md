## Answer

Based on the provided context, Lumbar fusion with lumbar decompression (indirect or direct) is considered medically necessary when performed for **ANY** of the following conditions, provided that **ALL** of the associated criteria for that specific condition have been met [5]:

1.  Actual Instability [6]
2.  Anticipated Iatrogenic Instability [7]
3.  Adult Symptomatic Degenerative Spinal Deformity [8]
4.  Initial Disc Herniation [9]
5.  Recurrent Disc Herniation [10]
6.  Second or Greater Recurrent Disc Herniation [11]

The detailed criteria for each qualifying condition are listed below.

### 1. Actual Instability

Lumbar fusion with decompression is medically necessary for Actual Instability when ALL of the following criteria are met [6]:

*   **Candidate Status:** The individual is a candidate for lumbar decompression or lumbar corpectomy per CMM-608: Lumbar Decompression [6].
*   **Imaging Findings:** Imaging findings include ANY of the following [6]:
    *   **Degenerative spondylolisthesis without spondylolysis** with EITHER of the following [6]:
        *   Dynamic segmental instability on flexion-extension plain x-rays OR comparison of a supine and upright image, with a difference in translational alignment between vertebrae >3mm between views [6].
        *   Meyerding Grade II or higher spondylolisthesis [6].
    *   **Spondylolisthesis with spondylolysis** (e.g., Isthmic Spondylolisthesis) with ANY of the following [6]:
        *   Meyerding Grade I or II spondylolisthesis (anterolisthesis) and plain x-rays support progression of anterolisthesis [6].
        *   Meyerding Grade III or higher spondylolisthesis (anterolisthesis) with ≥50% anterior slippage OR plain x-rays support progression of anterolisthesis [6].
    *   Progressive spinal pain without confirmatory imaging showing progression of spondylolisthesis [6].
    *   Multi-level spondylolysis on plain x-rays [6].
    *   Post-operative instability created by the disruption of the posterior elements due to facet joint excision that exceeding 50% bilaterally or 75% or more of a single facet [6].
    *   Pars fracture [6].
    *   Previous spinal decompression that resulted in iatrogenic spondylolisthesis [6].
    *   **Criteria Exception:** When instability is created and/or identified intra-operatively, the above imaging criteria are NOT required [6].
*   **Nicotine-Free Status:** Documentation of nicotine-free status with EITHER of the following [6]:
    *   Individual is a never-smoker [6].
    *   Individual has refrained from smoking, use of smokeless tobacco products, and/or nicotine replacement therapy for at least six (6) weeks prior to planned surgery validated by objective cotinine testing methods (serum, urinary, or saliva) verified as within the normal range for the testing method and lab at which the test was performed [6].

### 2. Anticipated Iatrogenic Instability

*   **Anticipated Iatrogenic Instability:** Anticipated iatrogenic instability with ANY of the following [7]:
    *   Created by disruption of the posterior elements due to facet joint excision exceeding 50% bilaterally or 75% or more of a single facet during spinal decompression [7].

#### Posterior Column Osteotomy (PCO)

Lumbar posterior column osteotomy (PCO) (i.e., Smith-Peterson osteotomy [SPO] or Ponte osteotomy) is considered medically necessary (in addition to fusion) when ALL of the following criteria have been met:

- Performed for non-fixed deformity requiring 5° to 10° of correction (SPO) per spinal segment with EITHER of the following:

- lumbar sagittal plane deformities where sagittal vertical axis (SVA) is &gt;8cm or pelvic incidence-lumbar lordosis (PI-LL) is &gt;15°

- larger coronal deformities where there is limited flexibility and the Cobb angle is &gt;30°

- Posterior column osteotomy is limited to a maximum of four (4) posterior column osteotomies performed in the apex of the deformity per correction surgery.

- Criteria exception : There is no limit to posterior column osteotomies for correction of Scheuermann's kyphosis as this deformity is long, gradual, rounded, and amendable to more than four (4) posterior column osteotomies

### Experimental, Investigational, or Unproven (EIU)

ALL of the following devices/procedures are considered experimental, investigational, or unproven (not an all-inclusive list):

- pre-sacral interbody fusion including AxiaLIF

- percutaneous lumbar spinal fusion (i.e., performed with indirect visualization of the spine)

- endoscopic lumbar spinal fusion

- anterior interbody fusion or implantation of intervertebral body fusion devices using laparoscopic approach

- device/implant not FDA approved

- dynamic (intervertebral) stabilization (e.g., Dynesys ® , Stabilimax NZ ® )

- interlaminar lumbar instrumented fusion (e.g., ILIF)

- interspinous and interlaminar distraction devices

- interspinous fixation/posterior non-pedicle supplemental fixation devices for spinal fusion (e.g., Affix ® , Aspen ®  Spinous Process Fixation System, Coflex ® -F)

- personalized anterior and lateral body interbody cage (implantable) (e.g., Aprevo ® )

- least invasive lumbar decompression interbody fusion (e.g., LINDIF)
