## Answer

Based on the provided context, lumbar fusion and lumbar osteotomy for pediatric spinal deformity are considered medically necessary when specific criteria regarding imaging findings and procedural requirements are met.

**Direct Answer**
Lumbar fusion is medically necessary for pediatric spinal deformity when imaging studies show the presence of specific deformities warranting definitive surgical treatment [15]. Lumbar osteotomy is medically necessary (in addition to fusion) when all criteria in the Osteotomy section (CMM-609.2) are met [16].

**Detailed Criteria for Pediatric Spinal Deformity**

**1. Pediatric Lumbar Fusion**
Lumbar fusion (arthrodesis) is considered medically necessary when the following criterion has been met [15]:
*   Imaging studies (advanced imaging or plain x-rays) show the presence of **ANY** of the following pediatric spinal deformities that warrant definitive surgical treatment [15]:
    *   Adolescent idiopathic scoliosis with Cobb angle >50° [15]
    *   Congenital scoliosis [15]
    *   Neuromuscular scoliosis [15]
    *   Infantile/juvenile scoliosis [15]

**2. Pediatric Osteotomy**
Lumbar osteotomy is considered medically necessary (in addition to fusion) when **ALL** of the criteria have been met in CMM-609.2: Osteotomy [16].

*Note: The specific criteria for CMM-609.2: Osteotomy are detailed below as they are referenced as a requirement for Pediatric Osteotomy.*

**Criteria for CMM-609.2: Osteotomy (Required for Pediatric Osteotomy)**

**A. Posterior Column Osteotomy (PCO)**
Lumbar posterior column osteotomy (PCO) (i.e., Smith-Peterson osteotomy [SPO] or Ponte osteotomy) is considered medically necessary (in addition to fusion) when **ALL** of the following criteria have been met [9]:
*   Performed for non-fixed deformity requiring 5° to 10° of correction (SPO) per spinal segment with **EITHER** of the following [9]:
    *   Lumbar sagittal plane deformities where sagittal vertical axis (SVA) is >8cm or pelvic incidence-lumbar lordosis (PI-LL) is >15° [9]
    *   Larger coronal deformities where there is limited flexibility and the Cobb angle is >30° [9]
*   Posterior column osteotomy is limited to a maximum of four (4) posterior column osteotomies performed in the apex of the deformity per correction surgery [9].
    *   **Criteria exception:** There is no limit to posterior column osteotomies for correction of Scheuermann's kyphosis as this deformity is long, gradual, rounded, and amendable to more than four (4) posterior column osteotomies [9].
*   Criteria have been met in the applicable procedure-specific section [9]:
    *   CMM-609.3: Pediatric Spinal Deformity [9]
    *   CMM-609.4: Lumbar Fusion (Arthrodesis) with Decompression (Indirect or Direct) [9]
    *   CMM-609.5: Lumbar Fusion (Arthrodesis) without Decompression [9]
    *   CMM-609.6: Adjacent Segment Disease [9]
    *   CMM-609.7: Lumbar Fusion (with or without Decompression) Following Failed Disc Arthroplasty Surgery [9]
    *   CMM-609.8: Repeat Lumbar Fusion (Arthrodesis) at the Same Level [9]

**B. Three-Column Osteotomy**
Lumbar three-column osteotomy (i.e., pedicle subtraction osteotomy (PSO) or vertebral column resection [VCR]) is considered medically necessary (in addition to fusion) when **ALL** of the following criteria have been met [10]:
*   Performed for **EITHER** of the following [10]:
    *   Fixed sagittal plane deformity requiring >30° of correction (PSO) [10]
    *   Large, fixed coronal deformities >60° that are amenable to asymmetric osteotomy [10]
*   Criteria have been met in the applicable procedure-specific section [10].
