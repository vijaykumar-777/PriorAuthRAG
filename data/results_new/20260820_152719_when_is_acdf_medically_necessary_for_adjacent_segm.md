Based on the provided context, specifically section **CMM-601.7: Adjacent Segment Disease** [4], Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) for adjacent segment disease is considered medically necessary when performed for EITHER of the following conditions (Radiculopathy or Myelopathy) when ALL of the associated criteria have been met:

**Radiculopathy** [4]
*   Greater than six (6) months since the prior cervical decompression or cervical fusion at an adjacent level [4]
*   Symptoms include BOTH of the following: [4]
    *   Significant level of pain on a daily basis defined as clinically significant functional impairment (e.g., inability to perform household chores, prolonged standing, etc.) [4]
    *   Unremitting radicular pain to shoulder girdle and/or upper extremity resulting in disability [4]
*   Physical exam findings include ANY of the following: [4]
    *   Dermatomal sensory deficit [4]
    *   Motor deficit (e.g., biceps, triceps weakness) [4]
    *   Reflex changes [4]
    *   Shoulder abduction relief sign [4]
    *   Nerve root tension sign (e.g., Spurling's maneuver) [4]
    *   Unremitting radicular pain to shoulder girdle and/or upper extremity without concordant objective physical exam findings [4]
*   Less than clinically meaningful improvement with at least TWO of the following (unless contraindicated): [4]
    *   Prescription strength analgesics, steroids, gabapentinoids, and/or nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) for six (6) weeks [4]
    *   Provider-directed exercise program (prescribed by a physical therapist, chiropractic provider, osteopathic or allopathic physician) for six (6) weeks [4]
    *   Epidural steroid injection(s) or selective nerve root block(s) performed at the same level(s) as the requested surgery [4]
*   Plain x-rays of the cervical spine (including flexion/extension lateral views) and advanced diagnostic imaging show successful decompression and fusion at the previous operative level [4]
*   MRI/CT shows neural structure compression at the requested level(s) that is concordant with the individual's symptoms and physical exam findings and that is caused by ANY of the following: [4]
    *   Herniated disc(s) (retained disc material or a recurrent disc herniation) [4]
    *   Synovial cyst or arachnoid cyst [4]
    *   Central/lateral/foraminal stenosis [4]
    *   Osteophytes [4]
*   Absence of unmanaged significant mental and/or behavioral health disorders (e.g., major depressive disorder, chronic pain syndrome, secondary gain, opioid and alcohol use disorders) [4]
*   Documentation of nicotine-free status with EITHER of the following: [4]
    *   Individual is a never-smoker [4]
    *   Individual has refrained from smoking, use of smokeless tobacco products, and/or nicotine replacement therapy for at least six (6) weeks prior to planned surgery validated by objective cotinine testing methods (serum, urinary, or saliva) verified as within the normal range for the testing method and lab at which the test was performed [4]

**Myelopathy** [4]
*   Symptoms include ANY of the following: [4]
    *   Upper/lower extremity weakness, numbness, or pain [4]
    *   Fine motor dysfunction (buttoning, handwriting, clumsiness of hands) [4]
    *   Gait disturbance [4]
    *   New-onset bowel or bladder dysfunction [4]
    *   Frequent falls [4]
*   Physical exam findings include ANY of the following: [4]
    *   Grip and release test [4]

*   MRI/CT shows findings that are concordant with the individual's symptoms and physical exam findings and that are caused by EITHER of the following: [4]
    *   Cervical spinal cord compression [4]

**CMM.SP.IN.601.8 v1.0.2026**

- **Failed Cervical Disc Arthroplasty Implant** • Post-operative imaging shows evidence of cervical disc arthroplasty implant malposition or failure (e.g., subsidence, loosening, infection, dislocation, subluxation, vertebral body fracture, dislodgement)

- **Unremitting Neck Pain** • Greater than six (6) months since the prior cervical total disc arthroplasty at the same level

- • Symptoms include significant level of pain on a daily basis defined as clinically significant functional impairment (e.g., inability to perform household chores, prolonged standing, etc.)

- • Post-operative physical exam findings are concordant with the individual's symptoms
