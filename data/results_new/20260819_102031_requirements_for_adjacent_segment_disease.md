# Query: requirements for adjacent segment disease

## Answer

According to the guidelines, Anterior Cervical Discectomy and Fusion (ACDF) for adjacent segment disease is considered medically necessary when performed for either **Radiculopathy** or **Myelopathy**, provided all associated criteria are met [3].

**General Requirements for Both Conditions:**
*   **Timeframe:** Greater than six (6) months must have passed since the prior cervical decompression or cervical fusion at an adjacent level [3].
*   **Imaging of Previous Surgery:** Plain x-rays of the cervical spine (including flexion/extension lateral views) and advanced diagnostic imaging must show successful decompression and fusion at the previous operative level [3].
*   **Mental Health:** There must be an absence of unmanaged significant mental and/or behavioral health disorders (e.g., major depressive disorder, chronic pain syndrome, secondary gain, opioid and alcohol use disorders) [3].
*   **Nicotine Status:** Documentation of nicotine-free status is required, defined as either:
    *   The individual is a never-smoker; OR
    *   The individual has refrained from smoking, use of smokeless tobacco products, and/or nicotine replacement therapy for at least six (6) weeks prior to planned surgery, validated by objective cotinine testing methods (serum, urinary, or saliva) verified as within the normal range [3].

**Specific Criteria for Radiculopathy:**
*   **Symptoms:** Must include BOTH:
    *   Significant level of pain on a daily basis defined as clinically significant functional impairment (e.g., inability to perform household chores, prolonged standing) [3].
    *   Unremitting radicular pain to the shoulder girdle and/or upper extremity resulting in disability [3].
*   **Physical Exam Findings:** Must include ANY of the following:
    *   Dermatomal sensory deficit [3].
    *   Motor deficit (e.g., biceps, triceps weakness) [3].
    *   Reflex changes [3].
    *   Shoulder abduction relief sign [3].
    *   Nerve root tension sign (e.g., Spurling's maneuver) [3].
    *   Unremitting radicular pain to shoulder girdle and/or upper extremity without concordant objective physical exam findings [3].
*   **Conservative Management:** Less than clinically meaningful improvement with at least TWO of the following (unless contraindicated):
    *   Prescription strength analgesics, steroids, gabapentinoids, and/or nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) for six (6) weeks [3].
    *   Provider-directed exercise program (prescribed by a physical therapist, chiropractic provider, osteopathic or allopathic physician) for six (6) weeks [3].
    *   Epidural steroid injection(s) or selective nerve root block(s) performed at the same level(s) as the requested surgery [3].
*   **Imaging of Requested Level:** MRI/CT must show neural structure compression at the requested level(s) that is concordant with symptoms and physical exam findings, caused by ANY of the following:
    *   Herniated disc(s) (retained disc material or a recurrent disc herniation) [3].
    *   Synovial cyst or arachnoid cyst [3].
    *   Central/lateral/foraminal stenosis [3].
    *   Osteophytes [3].

**Specific Criteria for Myelopathy:**
*   **Symptoms:** Must include ANY of the following:
    *   Upper/lower extremity weakness, numbness, or pain [3].
    *   Fine motor dysfunction (buttoning, handwriting, clumsiness of hands) [3].
    *   Gait disturbance [3].
    *   New-onset bowel or bladder dysfunction [3].
    *   Frequent falls [3].
*   **Physical Exam Findings:** Must include ANY of the following:
    *   Grip and release test [3].
    *   Ataxic gait [3].
    *   Hyperreflexia [3].
    *   Hoffmann sign [3].
    *   Babinski sign [3].
    *   Tandem walking test demonstrating ataxia [3].
    *   Inverted brachial radial reflex [3].
    *   Increased muscle tone or spasticity [3].
    *   Clonus [3].
    *   Myelopathic hand [3].
*   **Imaging of Requested Level:** MRI/CT must show findings concordant with symptoms and physical exam findings caused by EITHER:
    *   Cervical spinal cord compression [3].
    *   Cervical spinal stenosis [3].

### **Not Medically Necessary**

- Anterior cervical discectomy/corpectomy and fusion performed without meeting the criteria in the **<u>General Guidelines</u>** when applicable for urgent/emergent conditions) and the criteria in the applicable procedure-specific section(s) ( **<u>initial fusion</u>** , **<u>corpectomy</u>** <u>,</u> **<u>repeat fusion</u>** , **<u>adjacent segment disease</u>** <u>, or</u> **<u>fusion following failed disc arthroplasty</u>** <u>) is considered</u> **not medically necessary** .

- Anterior cervical discectomy/corpectomy and fusion performed for ANY other reason is considered **not medically necessary** including, but not limited to, performed for EITHER of the following conditions:

- chronic non-specific neck or arm pain of unknown etiology

- ◦ cervical degenerative disc disease without radiculopathy or myelopathy

- Anterior cervical discectomy/corpectomy performed alone (i.e., performed without a cervical fusion) is considered **not medically necessary** .
