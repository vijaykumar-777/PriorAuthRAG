# Query: when is repeat ACDF considered medically necessary

*Generated: 2026-08-28 17:07:43 | mode=keyword | alpha=0.5 | top_k=10*

## Answer

Repeat anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at the same level is considered medically necessary when performed for **ANY** of the following conditions, provided that **ALL** of the associated criteria for that specific condition have been met:

1.  Malposition or Failure of Implant/Instrumentation or Structural Bone Graft [6][7]
2.  Unremitting Neck Pain with Pseudoarthrosis [6][8]
3.  Radiculopathy with Pseudoarthrosis [6][9]
4.  Myelopathy with Pseudoarthrosis [6][10]

The detailed criteria for each qualifying condition are listed below.

### 1. Malposition or Failure of Implant/Instrumentation or Structural Bone Graft

*   Post-operative imaging shows evidence of malposition or failure of the implant/instrumentation or structural bone graft (e.g., migration, pedicle screw breakage, pedicle screw loosening, dislodged hooks, rod breakage, rod bending, rod loosening, loss of curve correction, decompensation, etc.) [7]

### 2. Unremitting Neck Pain with Pseudoarthrosis

*   Greater than six (6) months since the prior anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at the same level [8]
*   Symptoms include significant level of pain on a daily basis defined as clinically significant functional impairment (e.g., inability to perform household chores, prolonged standing, etc.) [8]
*   Post-operative physical exam findings are concordant with the individual's symptoms [8]
*   Less than clinically meaningful improvement with **BOTH** of the following (unless contraindicated):
    *   Prescription strength analgesics, steroids, gabapentinoids, and/or nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) for six (6) weeks [8]
    *   Provider-directed exercise program (prescribed by a physical therapist, chiropractic provider, osteopathic or allopathic physician) for six (6) weeks [8]
*   Post-operative imaging (performed at no less than six (6) months after the prior cervical fusion) shows pseudoarthrosis at the requested level(s) [8]
*   Post-operative MRI/CT findings are concordant with the individual's symptoms [8]
*   Absence of unmanaged significant mental and/or behavioral health disorders (e.g., major depressive disorder, chronic pain syndrome, secondary gain, opioid and alcohol use disorders) [8]
*   Documentation of nicotine-free status with **EITHER** of the following:
    *   Individual is a never-smoker [8]
    *   Individual has refrained from smoking, use of smokeless tobacco products, and/or nicotine replacement therapy for at least six (6) weeks prior to planned surgery validated by objective cotinine testing methods (serum, urinary, or saliva) verified as within the normal range for the testing method and lab at which the test was performed [8]

### 3. Radiculopathy with Pseudoarthrosis

*   Greater than six (6) months since the prior anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at the same level [9]
*   Symptoms include **BOTH** of the following:
    *   Significant level of pain on a daily basis defined as clinically significant functional impairment (e.g., inability to perform household chores, prolonged standing, etc.) [9]
    *   Unremitting radicular pain to shoulder girdle and/or upper extremity resulting in disability [9]
*   Physical exam findings include **ANY** of the following:
    *   Dermatomal sensory deficit [9]
    *   Motor deficit (e.g., biceps, triceps weakness) [9]
    *   Reflex changes [9]
    *   Shoulder abduction relief sign [9]
    *   Nerve root tension sign (e.g., Spurling's maneuver) [9]
    *   Unremitting radicular pain to shoulder girdle and/or upper extremity without concordant objective physical exam findings [9]
*   Less than clinically meaningful improvement with at least **TWO** of the following (unless contraindicated):
    *   Prescription strength analgesics, steroids, gabapentinoids, and/or nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) for six (6) weeks [9]
    *   Provider-directed exercise program (prescribed by a physical therapist, chiropractic provider, osteopathic or allopathic physician) for six (6) weeks [9]
    *   Epidural steroid injection(s) or selective nerve root block(s) performed at the same level(s) as the requested surgery [9]
*   Post-operative imaging (performed at no less than six (6) months after the prior cervical fusion) shows pseudoarthrosis at the requested level(s) [9]
*   Post-operative MRI/CT shows neural structure compression at the requested level(s) that is concordant with the individual's symptoms and physical exam findings and that is caused by **ANY** of the following:
    *   Herniated disc(s) (retained disc material or a recurrent disc herniation) [9]
    *   Synovial cyst or arachnoid cyst [9]
    *   Central/lateral/foraminal stenosis [9]
    *   Osteophytes [9]
*   Absence of unmanaged significant mental and/or behavioral health disorders (e.g., major depressive disorder, chronic pain syndrome, secondary gain, opioid and alcohol use disorders) [9]
*   Documentation of nicotine-free status with **EITHER** of the following:
    *   Individual is a never-smoker [9]
    *   Individual has refrained from smoking, use of smokeless tobacco products, and/or nicotine replacement therapy for at least six (6) weeks prior to planned surgery validated by objective cotinine testing methods (serum, urinary, or saliva) verified as within the normal range for the testing method and lab at which the test was performed [9]

### 4. Myelopathy with Pseudoarthrosis

*   Greater than six (6) months since the prior anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) at the same level [10]
*   Symptoms include **ANY** of the following:
    *   Upper/lower extremity weakness, numbness, or pain [10]
    *   Fine motor dysfunction (buttoning, handwriting, clumsiness of hands) [10]
    *   Gait disturbance [10]
    *   New-onset bowel or bladder dysfunction [10]
    *   Frequent falls [10]
*   Physical exam findings include **ANY** of the following:
    *   Grip and release test [10]
    *   Ataxic gait [10]
    *   Hyperreflexia [10]
    *   Hoffmann sign [10]
    *   Babinski sign [10]
    *   Tandem walking test demonstrating ataxia [10]
    *   Inverted brachial radial reflex [10]
    *   Increased muscle tone or spasticity [10]
    *   Clonus [10]
    *   Myelopathic hand [10]
*   Post-operative imaging (performed at no less than six (6) months after the prior cervical fusion) shows pseudoarthrosis at the requested level(s) [10]
*   Post-operative MRI/CT shows findings that are concordant with the individual's symptoms and physical exam findings and that are caused by **EITHER** of the following:
    *   Cervical spinal cord compression [10]
    *   Cervical spinal stenosis [10]
